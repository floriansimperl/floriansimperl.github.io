About Me

Who I am and what motivates me

I am an applied physicist working on computational methods for X-ray photoelectron (XP) and electron spectroscopy. My work includes materials modelling with machine-learned interatomic potentials, Monte Carlo simulations of electron transport in solids (NIST SRD 100 SESSA), deep learning models for quantitative XPS and data standardisation efforts for XPS and materials research in general.

I am especially passionate about making research data FAIR (findable, accessible, interoperable and reusable) to which end I have worked on data standardisation schemas with the FAIRmat consortium and implemented NeXus-based data standards in my own scientific analysis workflows and related publications. I want to make research data truly AI-ready, so we can use the full potential of novel AI models in science to enable new discoveries.

Besides my research work, I am passionate about mountaineering and being awestruck by the glaciers as long as they are still around. I also enjoy working on small Arduino projects and going out for wildlife and nature photography.
